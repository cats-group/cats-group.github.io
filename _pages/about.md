CATS (Computational Analysis of Text and Society) is an interdisciplinary research lab led by [Julia Mendelsohn](https://juliamendelsohn.github.io/) at the [University of Maryland College of Information](https://ischool.umd.edu/) and the [University of Maryland Institute for Advanced Computer Studies](https://www.umics.umd.edu/). We are also part of the broader [Computational Linguistics and Information Processing (CLIP) Lab](https://wiki.umiacs.umd.edu/clip/index.php/Main_Page).

We develop computational methods to study language, politics, and society. Our work combines natural language processing computational social science, and sociolinguistics to answer questions about the world around us. Much of our work concerns implicit meaning: how speakers convey ideas, signal group membership, and cause harm without stating anything outright. We are also interested in framing and persuasion, and in the linguistic strategies that shape how people understand issues and what they come to believe about them. A third strand is computational sociolinguistics, where we study how language use and variation in online communities relate to social identity.

We treat language technologies, including large language models, primarily as tools for understanding people: they help us find patterns across text at a scale that would otherwise be out of reach. We also consider LLMs as an object of study, especially in evaluating social biases these systems encode and their downstream implications.
